A few days ago I found myself thinking about a phrase that, although it sounds harsh, is quite true: in the modern world, if you're not on the Internet, you practically don't exist.

I don't say this out of vanity or an obsession with metrics. I say it because today, when someone looks for a solution, a collaborator, or an idea, the first place they look is at a screen. And if you're not there, you simply don't show up.

We all have something valuable to contribute. A project, an idea, a tool that could help someone else. But if we don't give it visibility, that value stays locked away in a digital drawer that no one opens.

And here comes the point I find fascinating and, at the same time, a little frustrating:

We live in the age of artificial intelligence. Thousands of people create weekend projects. Many of them are ingenious, sure, but they end up piling up as noise online. Posts that get lost, repositories no one sees, ideas that fade away.

Social networks try to help us: LinkedIn, dev.to, certain subreddits… and they're fine. But let's be honest: your projects get buried in a sea of posts within hours. And chances are they'll never show up when someone asks ChatGPT: "Is there a program that solves this need?"

There's the problem. And also the opportunity.

Because the major artificial intelligence providers constantly scan the web. They look for keywords, real value, projects that solve problems. And they don't care if you have 100 users or a million. They don't care how much you spend on advertising. They care about the value you provide.

I've seen small projects, with modest communities, appear in AI responses alongside industry giants. The difference? Those projects had a personal site where they clearly showcased what they did.

A personal site for your open source projects isn't a luxury. It's, perhaps, the most honest and effective way to tell the world: "This exists. This is what I do. This is what I can contribute."

It's not about having thousands of followers. It's about existing in the right place, with the right words, so that whoever needs you can find you. And so that the machines that now mediate so many searches also know you're there.

So if you have that project saved away, that idea you've been mulling over for months, that code that solves a real problem… take it out of your hard drive. Give it a home. Tell the world what it does. Not for noise, but for value.

Because in the end, the difference isn't made by what you pay. It's made by what you contribute.

And that deserves to be seen.
